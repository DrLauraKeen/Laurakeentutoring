Dr Laura Keen

A scholar in the room with your child

Laura's doctoral research examined the role of wild animals in Roman epic poetry — the lions, boars, and serpents that stalk the pages of Virgil and Statius — completed at the University of Exeter, where she also earned an MA in Classics and Ancient History with distinction. Her academic path began with a Scottish MA at the University of St Andrews. Her thesis, Wild Animals in Roman Epic, is available through the University of Exeter's research repository.

Since 2016 she has led Classics at The Maynard School in Exeter, teaching Latin at GCSE and A Level, Classics at GCSE and A Level, and — until recently — Ancient Greek at A Level, alongside supervising Sixth Form tutees and Extended Project Qualifications. She has also lectured on Ancient Greek for adult learners through the University of Exeter's Evening Languages Programme, and devised its "Step into Ancient Greek" course for sixth-formers across state, grammar, and independent schools.

Her chapter, "Animals in Epic," appears in the Oxford Handbook of Animals in Classical Thought (Oxford University Press, 2014), and in 2023 she spoke to BBC Radio Devon about widening access to Ancient Greek and Japanese at university level.

· WHY LATIN ·

Not a dead language — a way in

Latin sharpens the way a student reads English, unlocks half the vocabulary of half of Europe, and hands them the key to two thousand years of literature, law, and argument. Classical Civilisation does the same for ideas: it explains why the Parthenon, the Aeneid, and the Roman senate still shape how we build, tell stories, and govern.

Lessons are built around your goals — a GCSE grade, confidence before an A Level exam, or simply the pleasure of finally reading Ovid in the original.

My doctorate explored how Roman epic poets used wild animals to say things beyond the text. That's what I want every student to feel — that these texts are still doing something, if you know how to look.
— DR LAURA KEEN
